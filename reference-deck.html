// what_it_is · what_you_do

What cyberbullying is, and what you do

Cyberbullying is using the internet to be mean to someone so they feel bad or upset.

// four_moves · in this order

1 Do not hit back

Hitting back usually makes it worse.

2 Tell someone you trust

At home, or here in school.

3 Report it

To the game, the site or the app.

4 Block them

So they cannot reach you.

A stranger is usually best walked away from. Someone you know is better reported.

Anything dangerous, a threat or your private information put online, is always reported.

Be an upstander, not a bystander. Check the person is alright, and say plainly that this is not okay.

// brain_break · end_of_lap_1

Four Corners

  • Each corner of the room has a card suit symbol: Spades, Hearts, Diamonds, Clubs.
  • Everyone quietly chooses a corner to stand in.
  • The teacher draws a card, either from a deck of cards, or by scanning the QR code to use an online card drawing app.
  • The teacher announces the suit on the card (for example "Clubs!").
  • Everyone standing in that corner is out and must sit down.
  • The rest of the students choose a new corner for the next round.
  • Keep playing until only one student is left: they are the winner!

3 minutes   tap the card, no pack and no phone needed

// good_game · the_story

Good Game: the story and your four tools

The story starts at a bus stop before the summer break. Friends plan to level up together in Bloon Pop. One of them cannot play: they broke their controller after frustrating encounters with online opponents and cheaters. So the crew starts a Good Game Guide.

// four_tools

Ignore

Do nothing back.

Correct

Say what is true.

Mute

Stop seeing their chat.

Report

Send it to the game.

Six scenarios follow, built from the problems the publisher names: teammates sabotaging progress and cheating, too much distraction in the in-game chat, and inappropriate screen names and language.

The game also teaches you not to misuse them, for example reporting an opponent who wins fairly.

// brain_break · end_of_lap_2

Bang Circle

  • All students stand in a circle facing inward. One person (usually the teacher or chosen student) stands in the middle of the circle. They are the caller. The caller quickly says the name of a student in the circle.
  • That named student must immediately duck down (crouch or squat). At the same time, the two students on either side of the ducking student turn to face each other and shout "BANG!" while pointing finger guns.
  • The first to say "BANG!" wins the duel. The slower one is out and must sit down or leave the circle. If the student in the middle ducks too slowly or ducks when they are not named, they are out.
  • The last two students left can have a final dramatic "BANG" showdown in the centre of the circle.

3 minutes
