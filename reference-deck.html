Lesson 1: the role play

In teams of four you share one Minecraft world on your iPads and act out a chat that turns mean. All of it is pretend: the Instigator types only the lines we put on the board, and the rest of the team practises what to do about it.

Why: you feel it first, then you learn the moves that work.

// what_it_is · what_you_do

What cyberbullying is, and what you do

Cyberbullying is using the internet to be mean to someone so they feel bad or upset.

// four_moves · in this order

1 Do not hit back

Hitting back usually makes it worse.

2 Tell someone you trust

At home, or here in school.

3 Report it

To the game, the site or the app.

4 Block them

So they cannot reach you.

A stranger is usually best walked away from. Someone you know is better reported.

Anything dangerous, a threat or your private information put online, is always reported.

Be an upstander, not a bystander. Check the person is alright, and say plainly that this is not okay.

// good_game · the_story

Good Game: the story and your four tools

The story starts at a bus stop before the summer break. Friends plan to level up together in Bloon Pop. One of them cannot play: they broke their controller after frustrating encounters with online opponents and cheaters. So the crew starts a Good Game Guide.

// four_tools

Ignore

Do nothing back.

Correct

Say what is true.

Mute

Stop seeing their chat.

Report

Send it to the game.

Six scenarios follow, built from the problems the publisher names: teammates sabotaging progress and cheating, too much distraction in the in-game chat, and inappropriate screen names and language.

The game also teaches you not to misuse them, for example reporting an opponent who wins fairly.

// brain_break_bank · this_or_that

This or That

  • Students line up in a straight line across the middle of the room.
  • Teacher reads out two choices, like "Chocolate or Vanilla."
  • Students who choose the first option, step to the left.
  • Students who choose the second option, step to the right.
  • Call out fun or themed "This or That" pairs (for example "Beach or Mountains", "Sweet or Salty"). Give students 3 to 5 seconds to decide and step.

// brain_break_bank · four_corners

Four Corners

  • Each corner of the room has a card suit symbol: Spades, Hearts, Diamonds, Clubs.
  • Everyone quietly chooses a corner to stand in.
  • The teacher draws a card, either from a deck of cards, or by scanning the QR code to use an online card drawing app.
  • The teacher announces the suit on the card (for example "Clubs!").
  • Everyone standing in that corner is out and must sit down.
  • The rest of the students choose a new corner for the next round.
  • Keep playing until only one student is left: they are the winner!

3 minutes   tap the card, no pack and no phone needed

// brain_break_bank · boi_ing_7

Boi-ing 7

  • Students stand in a circle facing inwards.
  • A nominated student begins by saying "one".
  • The student to the left of the one says "two" and so on around the circle, calling out numbers in numerical order.
  • Every time a number comes along that contains a "7" or is a multiple of 7, students must call out "boi-ing" (a jumping sound because "7" is being jumped) in place of the actual number. Counting then continues as normal until the next "7".

3 minutes

// brain_break_bank · look_down_look_up

Look Down, Look Up

  • Students stand in a circle facing inwards.
  • Groups of approximately 10 to 12 are ideal.
  • Students stare at a small object placed on the floor in the middle of the circle.
  • Each student decides on a person that they will directly look at when instructed to "Look Up".
  • If they find that this person has also chosen them and is looking directly at them, then both students step out of the circle. After each turn, students are asked to "Look Down".
  • They return their gaze to the floor and decide whom they will look at as they await the next call of "Look Up". Play continues until only one or two players remain.

3 minutes

// brain_break_bank · bang_circle

Bang Circle

  • All students stand in a circle facing inward. One person (usually the teacher or chosen student) stands in the middle of the circle. They are the caller. The caller quickly says the name of a student in the circle.
  • That named student must immediately duck down (crouch or squat). At the same time, the two students on either side of the ducking student turn to face each other and shout "BANG!" while pointing finger guns.
  • The first to say "BANG!" wins the duel. The slower one is out and must sit down or leave the circle. If the student in the middle ducks too slowly or ducks when they are not named, they are out.
  • The last two students left can have a final dramatic "BANG" showdown in the centre of the circle.

3 minutes

// brain_break_bank · heads_or_tails

Heads or Tails

  • Everyone stands up in the middle of the room.
  • The teacher says, "Choose heads or tails!" Students show their guess using an action: Heads, hands on your head, or Tails, hands on your hips.
  • The teacher flips the coin, either using a real coin, or by scanning the QR code and flipping virtually.
  • The teacher calls out the result (for example "Tails!").
  • Students who guessed wrong sit down.
  • The rest stay standing and get ready for the next round.
  • Keep playing until only one student is left standing: they are the winner!

3 minutes   tap the coin, no real coin or phone needed

// brain_break_bank · silent_ball

Silent Ball

  • Equipment: one medium size ball, any that is appropriate for the players' catching skill level (a soft or squishy ball in a classroom).
  • How to play: the leader counts down, "3, 2, 1, silent" and passes the ball to another person in the play area.
  • A player must sit down if they drop the ball, make a bad pass, or talk or make noise.
  • Play continues until only one person remains. The last player standing gets to be first to throw the ball in the next round.

3 minutes

// brain_break_bank · heads_down_thumbs_up

Heads Down, Thumbs Up

  • 1. All players sit with their heads down on their arms and eyes closed. One thumb is raised.
  • 2. The leader chooses 2 to 4 Tappers (those at the front know who they are; others do not).
  • 3. Tappers walk quietly around the room and each tap one player's thumb gently.
  • 4. Tappers return to the front of the room.
  • 5. The leader gives the signal: everyone lifts their heads and opens their eyes.
  • 6. Each tapped player stands. One at a time, they guess which Tapper touched them.
  • Correct guess: the Guesser and Tapper swap roles. Incorrect guess: the Tapper stays in their role for the next round.

10 minutes
